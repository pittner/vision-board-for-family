12 questions to ask your kids before you choose

Go around the table, youngest first. Parents answer too. Write down every answer that makes the others nod: those are your family goals.

  1. What was your favorite day with our family in 2026?
  2. What made you really proud of yourself this year?
  3. What do you want to learn or get better at next year?
  4. Where would you love to go together in 2027?
  5. What is one thing we should do more often as a family?
  6. What is one thing we should do less often?
  7. What does a perfect family Saturday look like for you?
  8. Which family tradition do you never want to stop?
  9. What new tradition should we start?
  10. Who would you like to see more often?
  11. How can we help each other when someone has a bad day?
  12. What is one wish you have for our family next year?

Make it as a family in 45 minutes

  1. 10 minutes: look back. Everyone, youngest first, names a favorite moment of 2026. Parents only listen and write.
  2. 15 minutes: ask the questions. Go around the table with the 12 questions below. Little kids can draw their answer instead of saying it.
  3. 10 minutes: pick three family goals. One for time together, one for home or health, one just for fun. Let the kids pick at least one of them.
  4. 10 minutes: make it visible. Turn your favorite goal and its first step into a phone lock screen, print it for the fridge, or make a full board together.

Questions

What is a family vision board?

A board of the goals your family shares for the coming year, such as more time together, a trip, a calmer home or a new skill. You make it together with your kids, so it doubles as a conversation about what everyone wants.

How do you make a family vision board with kids?

Look back at the year together, ask each child a few simple questions, pick three family goals and make them visible. This page has 36 family goals with a first step, 12 questions kids can answer and a 45-minute plan.

What should a family put on a vision board?

Goals the whole family cares about: time together, home, health and outdoors, learning, travel and kindness. Add one first step to each goal so the board says what to do this week, not only what to dream about.

What age is a family vision board for?

From about four years up. Younger kids draw or pick pictures, older kids write their own goals and choose the first steps.
